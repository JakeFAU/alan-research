# Phase 3C.3: continuation interfaces and possible completion

## Question and result

Can the full inherited capacity/custody snapshot support replacement when a
continuation constrains future ordering? **No:** the frozen snapshot candidate
forgets future transition structure. Can an explicit behavioral interface
preserve possible completion? **Conditionally:** equal marked finite visible
languages preserve initial existential CanComplete under the declared product
rules. This is continuation-monitor substitution over the unchanged whole K,
which still contains B's service semantics, not replacement of a complete agent.

The [frozen argument](01-argument.md) separates its mathematical premises from
finite implementation evidence. Its final pending-Task-2 wording records the
historical capture boundary; the [accepted independent audit](03-independent-audit.md)
and [qualification](05-qualification.md) give the subsequent executed status.
No frozen source or prior evidence has been rewritten to remove that chronology.

## Negative evidence retained

Histories 1/2 genuinely execute commit AB, send-request AB, admit AB and reserve
AB under early/shared-1/mask0. Their entire old concrete state, configuration,
capabilities, exact holds and static service/release rules agree at that cut.
AB is W and held; BC is U and unheld; AB's service guard is true. Commit-first
requires BC commitment before AB application. That removes AB's service readiness
while BC cannot reserve Q. Release-first can apply AB before committing BC,
release Q and finish. Full product reverse reachability, not the sampled stalled
path alone, establishes the opposite CanComplete labels.

Release-first, release-first-tau and release-first-doomed have equal initial
marked languages. The doomed branch nevertheless reaches unpaid debt with no
completion suffix. Histories 7/8 preserve its successful and doomed choices;
crash/recovery in history 8 retains PC 3 and its hold. Every state of every M,
including the doomed sink, is marked: the negative result is not a manufactured
private acceptance failure. Initial language equality does not preserve every
residual language, universal nonblocking, branching, refusals, fairness,
inevitability, divergence or exact cost.

The old stable-ready theorem applies to unrestricted K. Added continuation
restrictions remove progress actions: reserve-AB-before-commit-BC can conflict
with complete-BC-before-reserve-AB for serial B. Its old every-reachable-state
conclusion therefore cannot be imported into K/M unchanged.

## Conditional argument and semantic boundary

Under exact signatures, one common visible synchronization alphabet, independent
private state and conjunctive marks, projecting successful finite product paths
and interleaving finite private segments in the reverse direction establish
`Lm(X || Y) = Lm(X) ∩ Lm(Y)`. Hence equal pointed M/N languages preserve initial
existential CanComplete in every compatible finite K, including K/D contexts.
Residual replacement requires equality of the particular residual languages.
Flattening both triple parenthesizations preserves the complete relation and
marks with one K and one Q relation. This is elementary automata reasoning,
not a new general process theorem or a mechanized proof.

M restricts only reserve/apply AB and commit BC; its other five events self-loop.
D restricts only commit AB and apply BC; its other six events self-loop. External
vetoes are rejected. A private tau changes only private state. Every application
is visible and keeps its exact binding. Visible steps jointly update factors
in the abstract relation; no distributed synchronization implementation is
qualified. Necessity using exact finite-word testers requires a larger grammar;
those testers are not established for the role-restricted ALAN context grammar.

Concrete wrapped steps project to product steps or stutters. The conditional
lifting argument reuses the inherited reachable-custody premises: transport
preparation retains M; private execution requires B up, with recovery first if
needed. Atomic application/result retention/Q release remains assumed. The eight
histories independently replay actual model results and retained control, but
are not exhaustive concrete-product, endpoint or runtime qualification.
