# Phase 3B closure assessment

**Verdict: close the bounded Phase 3B investigation.** The original exit evidence exists, and every original progression question has been attacked at the evidence tier stated below. This is closure of a research investigation yielding positive results, counterexamples and characterized obstructions. It is not a theorem that two individually correct durable agents compose correctly. No original mandatory question remains wholly unexamined; several stronger positive properties remain unestablished.

The authority for this decision is the unchanged question, exit-evidence table and progression-gate paragraphs in the [Phase 3 roadmap](../phase3/README.md), interpreted under the [closure contract](00-contract.md). In particular, “attack” and “correspondence to an implemented fragment” do not require every candidate transition to succeed or a single refinement covering all schedules. The decision would change to **not established** if closure were taken to assert successful cross-generation delegation, delegated reciprocal custody under cancellation after admission, symmetric runtime crash coverage, or whole-pair completion with non-atomic effects. None is asserted here.

This audit began from merged research base `7ae2c5ddd5e18d2907dd91fcefc896456a25562d`, with Task 1 starting at `b0a3b2a1`. It ran no model, runtime campaign, checker qualification or Go suite and opened no retained SQLite. All experimental counts below are historical packet results. The new evidence consists of source inspection, selected raw JSON inspection and byte/hash comparisons; see [verification](02-verification.md), [input hashes](evidence/inspected-inputs.json) and [extracted facts](evidence/inspected-facts.json). Reconnaissance is a source map, not an independent proof of closure.

## Five closure hazards and their resolution

**Identity.** Rejecting Retry is an actual obstruction of the chosen composed public surface, not evidence that a hypothetical new generation preserves delegation. The fixed-generation implemented fragment satisfies the exit's fragment requirement; the positive extension would require a genuine successor/revision writer and stale-grant/custody attacks across that transition. G2 cannot be cited as such qualification.

**Cancellation.** Phase 2B's A-source traces supply genuine cancellation after physical admission. In the archived 3B.4 source, `dispatch.go:119–146` checks current request authority before registering the ordinary opportunity; `external.go:204–208` goes from the admitted/pre-call hook directly to `client.Do`. This supports only the source-level inference that later logical cancellation adds no post-admission physical fence. It does not lift the entire old qualification through the new delegation machinery. Exact-root revocation in N06/N07 and 3B.5 is a different operation; it preserves historical allowance while denying fresh admission and can leave an older opportunity usable. For a positive delegated-cancellation result, the minimum additional experiment would hold an authentic request-bound service after its allowed gate, perform actual CancelRun/RetireSubject, then check contact, retained result and both unpaid original dispositions under the pinned version. That experiment is unnecessary to claim the already witnessed adverse boundary, and has not been performed here.

**Server roles.** The roadmap's “B's own effect/receipt crash window” denotes the serving component in its original delegation question. The late selected BA case instantiates that role with A; endpoint B is not the serving ALAN agent. This audit counts the role-specific boundary and does not substitute a symmetry theorem for missing runtime coverage of the opposite service. The reciprocal models do contain both roles, under their different atomic premise.

**Atomic versus split domains.** `mutualobligations.Successors` sets `Applied`, `Retained` and `WorldResult` together. 3B.5 inherits that transition. Its safe-suffix predicate is exactly `Unsafe==0 && BA.Decision!=Refused && (!Revoked || BA.Applied || BA.Permit)` on its reachable finite graph. `effectobservation.Successors` changes application separately from response, retention and consumption; owner loss clears the opportunity/response but not a launched packet or world fact. Therefore the later one-service results cannot strengthen the earlier whole-pair predicate into a split-effect completion theorem. They instead expose why the atomic premise matters.

**Quiescence.** Complete local exports and final reports match in the two 3B.7 worlds, but complete forensic dossiers differ. A decision restricted to the declared local input cannot classify the differing application fact correctly in both witnessed worlds. This is a conditional observation argument with honest source/process/endpoint assumptions, not an unrestricted impossibility theorem. It establishes neither a distributed termination detector nor a local-to-global implication.

## Preserved negative history and remaining work

The original serial trap, authority consent/tuple-invention failures, v1 selection-order mismatch, sticky refusals, unpaid auxiliary originals, unsupported new-invocation/revision transitions, generic unknown-result non-retransmission and atomic-projection exclusions remain intact. Prior review rejections remain in their owning packets. In particular, [3B.7 initial reports](../phase3b7/evidence/qualification/initial-checks.json) rejected both captures; the [root-cause record](../phase3b7/evidence/task2/review-r2/root-cause.json) shows the passive authorization-coordinate error. Corrected checking accepted the unchanged sealed pairs. The initial capture was not initially accepted, and this audit does not rerun that checker.

**Phase 3C's original obligations remain open:** define a composition operator and observable equivalence; show interface assumptions survive hiding and regrouping with explicit associativity conditions; attack a three-agent cycle and shared-authority fan-out. No reduction in verification cost follows before that proof or counterexample.

**Optional extensions beyond this closure domain remain unestablished:** successful invocation/ownership changes; delegated cancellation-after-admission custody; opposite-service runtime loss coverage; whole-pair split-effect progress; independent-owner distributed recovery; reverse/all-state refinement; general shared-resource semantics; arbitrary endpoint/retry/import behavior; universal liveness under unbounded loss. These are precise limits on reuse of the result, not a demand to implement all extensions before closing this bounded investigation.
