## Finding

The useful research question is whether a component contract preserves **one shared accounting root and its remaining obligations** when authority fans out to two distinct services. Set attenuation alone cannot answer that question. Existing ALAN evidence already distinguishes reusable permission from resource expenditure and already tests shared-root reservation conservation. A proposed negative model should therefore falsify an insufficient composition rule, not invent a current runtime bug or advertise double-spending as a novel result.

An especially important boundary: Phase 3B.3 charges a delegated effect to **the serving principal's existing resource root**, not automatically to its permission issuer's resource root (`docs/foundational/phase3b3/02-contract.md:28`). Sharing a permission ancestor does not entail sharing a budget. The new experiment must explicitly place both serving principals in the same actual accounting root, or explicitly introduce a new research-only consumable-right contract. Do not infer that two separately provisioned recipient budgets spend the issuer's budget.

## Smallest useful attack

Use exactly three logical principals A, B, C at generation one, two immutable originals eB=(A,B,requestB,serviceB) and eC=(A,C,requestC,serviceC), two one-hop exact grants from A, and one accounting root R of limit one. Explicitly declare B and C as members of R. An authority root P owned by A permits the same full action tuple in both grants. Keep P (authority provenance) distinct in type from R (consumable accounting scope), even if a fixture associates them. Result values may coincide; originals and reservations must remain distinct. Keep B/C servicing so there is no circular-wait confound.

**Candidate bad composition rule:** each pair summary says “root R, cap 1, my debit count 0; my exact grant is valid”, and independently reserves its local original. After eB and eC reserve, each pair satisfies its local `debit <= 1` test and tuple attenuation, while the merged history has two distinct allowed reservations against the same R. Both effects/receipts can then be locally authentic while global conservation has already failed. Record the violation at the second reservation rather than waiting for final counters. No concurrency or crash is required; serially composing stale independent summaries suffices. This is a basic duplicated-allowance counterexample, not a new double-spending theorem.

Two useful information-loss variants sharpen what the contract must expose:

- Hide B's reservation while exporting only R's identity/cap and C's unchanged local state. Compare a reachable prefix where B has not reserved to one where B has reserved; C's local grant and original are equal, but whether C may reserve differs. A root identity and copied static description are insufficient without shared consumption evidence. Explicitly include the root interface among observations when claiming equivalence; the positive contract intentionally distinguishes these prefixes.
- Reserve eB, lose B's physical opportunity through crash, recover B at a new activation, and clone/reinitialize “remaining=1”. A fresh eC reservation violates conservation even if B never applied. If B's external result is unknown in a separate split-effect negative scenario, lack of a receipt cannot justify a refund. This is a candidate bad interface/recovery rule, not alleged behavior of current root-budget code.

Necessary positive controls: cap two permits both distinct services under sufficient other resources; cap one admits at most one and retains the other's genuine refusal/unpaid debt; two genuinely distinct roots with identical descriptors remain two roots and must not be incorrectly merged; repeated delivery of one exact original reuses its reservation, while equal payloads in eB/eC remain two charges. Duplicate permission grants alone are not a resource-conservation violation when the permissions are reusable.

## Bounded positive target

Prefer a research-only finite contract-preservation result over new runtime infrastructure:

- Fixed A/B/C fan-out grammar, two originals, fixed generations, explicit one-hop grants, authentic communication and immutable retained state. No redelegation, transfer, dynamic membership, hostile storage or distributed writers.
- One authoritative root accounting record with a stable namespace-bound identity, immutable limit, and a monotone set of exact original reservation identities. Root membership is resolved against retained declarations, not trusted from a copied `RootID` string or equality of descriptions.
- Reservation is the single atomic transition that checks current required authority, source/closure eligibility, exact binding and root capacity, then inserts the immutable decision plus one unique charge. Repeated reservation delivery reuses that decision. Physical admission is separately checked as in the earlier authority work; it cannot create, duplicate or refund a charge. Every application links to the original allowed reservation and appropriate live activation opportunity.
- A component join unifies shared-root **references**, not independent budget allocations. Shared records must agree on immutable root/limit/membership; globally compatible histories are restrictions of one root decision order. It may not union arbitrary independently generated local histories merely because each is individually under its cap. Ownership stays distinct from read-only references: no duplicated root owner on regrouping. Preserve named obligations, grants, selected paths, decisions/charges, per-agent availability, and result custody through both parenthesizations.
- Crashes change physical availability/activation and lose volatile opportunities only; the root debit set, original bindings, first refusals and real receipts remain. No refund, rebinding, new root or new grant is inferred from recovery, revocation or caller cancellation.

Proposed source-level safety proposition: under these fixed premises, every reachable grouped state has the same root reservation set and named obligations as its flat interpretation; `|charged(R)| <= limit(R)`; every application has exact authority/reservation/admission ancestry; joins/hiding preserving the root interface preserve these properties. Prove by transition induction, including rejected/repeated reservations and crash/recovery. This instantiates established resource-accounting reasoning in ALAN's specific contract; do not claim new general capability or distributed-consensus theory.

For progress, make a separate, narrow claim: under permanent covering authority, no historical refusal, capacity for all remaining distinct reservations, servicing B/C, recoverable owners, sufficient other resources, and atomic application/receipt retention, each declared original has a finite completion suffix; grouping preserving the full shared-root state preserves that existence. At cap one with two unit-cost unpaid originals, all-service completion is impossible by the explicit accounting premise, even while authority safety holds. A refusal is an accounted decision, not successful service. Do not turn one winner's completion into pair completion or a scheduler fairness theorem.

Keep split non-idempotent effect/receipt loss as an explicitly separate boundary/control, not a hidden relaxation of the positive premise. It may preserve charges and authority while leaving service completion unknown or blocked. Reusing Phase 3B.6/3B.7 observation results as historical context is enough; no new endpoint/recovery platform is needed.

The meaningful preservation claim is more than equality of canonical set unions. If asserting a concrete/abstract completion equivalence, provide a representative-wise lifting argument that accounts for the shared reservation gate and explain exactly which concrete transition classes it covers. Otherwise label the result only a finite safety model plus grouping invariant, not a full extension of Phase 3C.1's abstraction theorem.

## Recommendation and risks

Recommended question: **What shared-root accounting information must survive composition so that two individually authorized services remain globally budget-safe, and under what separate capacity/receipt assumptions can both complete?** The smallest packet is the fixed three-agent fan-out, one shared root, bounds one/two, copied-allowance and hidden-debit counterexamples, retained-charge recovery, a conditional flat/grouped safety argument and independent finite audit. No runtime change is required.

Main risks are semantic conflation (permission origin versus serving budget), falsely importing Phase 3C.1's disjoint-resource/one-outgoing grammar, using resource impossibility as an authority defect, calling an unknown outcome unspent, and presenting a familiar accounting invariant as mathematical novelty. Source inspection supports this direction; it does not establish the proposed model's results. No new outcome counts or tests are claimed.

## Follow-up assessment: prefer the conserved reusable-capacity witness

The parent supplied a stronger candidate after the preceding reconnaissance. It should be the primary packet if the target includes progress composition: **conservation can remain true while a shared reusable permit defeats an otherwise acyclic service-dependency argument**. The copied consumable-allowance case above then becomes a distinction/control, not the headline result.

Fix the existing chain originals e=A→B and f=B→C, B serial and C servicing, and one reusable capacity root Rcap shared by their services. Use exact static original-bound authority that already covers e and f. Freeze all cumulative effect budgets at sufficient capacity or leave them outside this small model. A capacity token is not a permission tuple and not a spent external-effect debit: it can be held and later released under an explicit rule, while a cumulative charge is never refunded merely by release.

The original-service topology is a **chain**, while references to Rcap fan out to multiple serving owners. Do not rename this into an A→B/A→C service fan-out. In particular, do not give f=B→C an A-issued request-bound grant and claim it satisfies the old issuer=requester rule. This experiment needs no new delegation protocol; shared accounting scope and authority provenance remain separately typed.

Candidate transitions: Commit moves U→W without needing the token. Acquire assigns the unique token to an original at W. Apply needs that original's token, the previous serial/servicing service guard, and an authentic current physical opportunity; application/receipt retention are atomic. Choose a precise release boundary before freezing the model; the simplest is atomic release with Apply. Receipt delivery/recording/consumption, commitment and endpoint recovery must remain independently serviceable. A stricter release-after-consumption model also supports the proposed adverse trace but adds custody to resource occupation and should not be smuggled into the first model.

At stages (e,f)=(W,W), free=0 and held=1:

- Holder=e: Apply(e) waits for f=D because B is serial. Acquire(f) waits for the token held by e. Neither original is applied, so there is no retained result to consume. This state has no completion suffix in the frozen no-release-before-apply relation, despite exact authority, intact custody, one-token conservation and an acyclic original service-dependency graph e→f.
- Holder=f: C can apply f, release its token, and deliver a real result that B consumes. B then stops waiting; e can acquire/apply/consume. This state has a completion suffix.

These states agree on U/W/P/D stages, physical availability and free/held **totals**, but differ on exact token holder. A progress contract exporting occupancy totals while hiding holder-to-original incidence loses a decisive fact. This is stronger than violating a spending invariant: the adverse state satisfies the chosen conservation invariant. It still does not falsify Phase 3C.1, whose disjoint/sufficient-resource assumptions exclude the new resource wait.

A one-time readiness check at Acquire does not repair it. Reach e=W,f=U; B is not yet waiting, so the previous guard permits Acquire(e). Then Commit(f) makes B wait, giving the dead state above. Preserve this trace as an independently reachable candidate failure; do not silently disable Commit(f). It exposes the temporal instability of “not currently waiting,” not an invented revocation or weakened authority check.

### Preferred bounded repair/positive target

Use **stable readiness** for acquisition in this research relation: the callee is permanently servicing, has no outgoing original in the frozen topology, or every fixed outgoing dependency of that callee is already D. Under fixed topology/policies, D absorbing, and no new generations/outgoing work, this predicate cannot become false through another Commit. For the chain, f can acquire immediately at W because C services; e can acquire only after f=D because B is serial. Commit remains unrestricted as in the prior grammar. This is a narrow admission discipline, not a proposed production scheduler/platform change.

An alternative discipline forbidding a holder from later committing a dependency could also avoid the trace, but changes ordinary commitment behavior and adds an obligation to police every dependency-introducing transition. Stable readiness is easier to falsify and prove for the fixed grammar. Do not generalize it to dynamic outgoing work or policy changes without a new premise/transition rule.

Proposed argument, still unexecuted/unproved here: (1) the single token is either free or held by exactly one named original, with no duplicate acquisition or invented release; (2) every holder was stably serviceable when it acquired and remains so in all later reachable states before Apply; (3) recover its physical endpoints and choose no further crashes, then that holder can realize a finite Apply/retention/release suffix using only its actual authority/custody; (4) when free, commit and finish the dependency-minimal original f, then e. This yields existence of completion from every reachable state in the fixed nonrevoking chain under reliable eventual selected delivery, recoverable owners and atomic application/receipt. The finite completion proof must account explicitly for any request/admission preparation before Apply; resource availability alone cannot invent request custody.

For crash modeling, retain exact capacity ownership across crash and renew only activation opportunities. Because the positive model's application/retention/release is atomic, a held token unambiguously represents unapplied work; recovery can continue that same original subject to fresh gate checks. Releasing the token just because an owner dies is a different policy and needs a non-overlap argument; it is unsafe to carry such a refund rule into split unknown external work. Revocation or permanent loss can preserve capacity conservation while defeating completion and should be excluded from the positive progress claim or reported as separate legitimate blocking profiles.

The composition interface must retain Rcap identity/capacity, exact original holder, acquisition/release order and root ownership/reference distinction, in addition to named stages/dependencies, authority witnesses and physical availability. Flat/grouped relations should agree after root identity unification; a copied slot pool and a hidden-holder summary are separate negative candidates. Regrouping must not create independent acquisition decisions against clones of one Rcap. Independent audit should reconstruct token ownership from events, reject nonholder release/stale-epoch application/double acquisition, independently regenerate complete small graphs and completion classifications, and validate both WW witnesses plus the Acquire(e)-then-Commit(f) trace.

Runtime boundary: Phase 1G explicitly distinguishes waiting logical invocations from active physical work and says waiting parents reserve no dispatcher slots (`docs/foundational/phase1g/01-composition-contract.md:101-105`). Its actual qualified capacity tests are not this new resource-holding relation. Nothing in the proposed witness establishes that the current runtime reserves a physical slot for a service whose serial dependency subsequently blocks it. The packet should characterize a candidate composition contract and its repair, with no claimed current runtime deadlock or new runtime campaign.
